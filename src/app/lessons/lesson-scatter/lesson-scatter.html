Linked scatter plot

The view switch in the header has a third option, Scatter: every country as a dot, GDP per person across, CO₂ per person up, dot area by population, coloured like the map. It's a third view of the same countries, sharing the selection, the year and the colours with the globe and the map: click a dot and the panel opens; switch to the globe and it flies there. Open it with the USA selected.

1. What it shows

  • 164 countries in 2022. On log axes they fall along a line: the correlation of log GDP and log CO₂ per person is 0.91, and the slope is about 1. Roughly, twice as rich, twice the emissions.
  • The interesting countries are off the line. Switzerland ($64k) and Sweden ($47k) emit 3.7 and 3.5 t per person; the USA ($57k) and Australia ($51k) emit 14.8 and 14.7 t. Similar wealth, four times the CO₂: energy mix and policy, not income alone.
  • The map can't show this: it colours one variable. The scatter plot puts two side by side, and the link back to the map says where each dot is.

2. Honest axes and sizes

  • Log axes: GDP runs from ~$700 to ~$120k per person, CO₂ from 0.03 t to 40 t. On linear axes nearly every country would crowd into the bottom-left corner. On log axes equal distances are equal ratios. A log axis must never be a surprise: here the tick labels say it (each one is ten times the last: 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10). A note under the chart said so too at first; it was dropped to keep the chart clean.
  • Ticks only at powers of 10 ($1k, $10k, $100k); the in-between ones bunch up on a log axis. The domain is the data plus a 25% margin, not .nice(), which on log axes rounds out to whole powers of 10 and left half the plot empty.
  • Area, not radius: scaleSqrt for the dot radius, so area ∝ population. India has ~4 times the people of the USA and gets a dot twice as wide. A linear radius would make it look 16 times bigger.
  • Biggest dots drawn first, so small countries sit on top and stay hoverable.
  • One year for both axes: GDP ends in 2022, CO₂ in 2024. The chart never mixes a 2022 GDP with 2024 emissions; past 2022 it stays on 2022, and its title says so ("Wealth × CO₂ in 2022") while the map shows 2024. It follows the timeline before that.

3. Linking two views: one shared store

<app-scatter-plot [(hovered)]="state.hovered" [selected]="state.selected()" (pick)="state.select($event)" />
<app-flat-map     [(hovered)]="state.hovered" [selected]="state.selected()" (selectedChange)="state.select($event)" />
<app-globe        [(hovered)]="state.hovered" … />
  • No view knows about the others. Each reads and writes the same two signals in ExplorerState (lesson 07): hovered and selected. Linking a third view is one more binding.
  • model() makes hovered two-way, the same pattern as the globe and the map.
  • Hover uses pointerenter / pointerleave on each dot: the browser hit-tests the circles, so there's no need for a quadtree or a Voronoi diagram at 164 points.
  • The marked dots (hovered, selected) are drawn again on top with a ring and a name. The rings ignore the pointer, so they never steal the hover from the dot underneath. Names flip to the left near the right edge.

4. A window first, then a view of its own

  • The first version put the chart in lesson 01's FloatingPanel, over the globe, opened by its own header button. Both views were on screen together, so hovering a dot lit the country up on the globe at the same moment: the linking was visible.
  • It became a third view, ?view=scatter, beside Globe and Map in the same switch. The chart gets the whole stage to read in, and there's one place to open it instead of two (a switch and a window). The cost: only one view is on screen, so hover linking across views isn't visible any more. Selection still is: it survives switching views.
  • The code barely changed, which is the point of section 3: ScatterView reads and writes the same store as before; only where it's drawn moved. View grew a third member, and the WebGL fallback only applies to the globe.
  • The plot follows its box with a ResizeObserver on its host; the scales' ranges come from that box, so a resized window redraws at the new size instead of stretching the text. That made the move from a window to a full stage free.

Review checklist

  • Press Scatter in the view switch: the chart fills the stage; the dots run bottom-left to top-right, coloured like the map
  • Hover a big dot: the readout names it, with a thin ring and its name on the chart
  • Click a dot: the country is selected (heavy ring, the panel opens); switch to the globe: it flies there
  • Find Switzerland and the USA: similar wealth, very different heights
  • Drag the timeline to 1990: the dots move; at 2024 the title stays on 2022 (GDP's last year)
  • Resize the browser: the plot redraws at the new size
