The divide view

The metric select in the legend has a new last option, The divide: responsibility × vulnerability. It colours every country by two things at once: how much of all CO₂ since 1750 it emitted, and how vulnerable it is to climate change. Open it on the map.

1. What it shows

  • In 2024, the 42 countries that are highly responsible and little vulnerable (pink: the USA, Canada, Europe, Russia, China, Australia, Brazil…) emitted 80.8% of all CO₂ since 1750.
  • The 38 that are little responsible and highly vulnerable (teal: Chad, Niger, Burkina Faso, Burundi, the Central African Republic…) emitted 0.09%, about a nine-hundredth as much.
  • Six countries are high on both (dark blue-purple): Iraq, North Korea, Libya, Nigeria, Pakistan and Vietnam. Five are low on both (light grey): Iceland, Malta, Barbados, Saint Lucia, Andorra.
  • That is the "carbon divide" this whole project is named after: those who caused the problem and those who will bear it are, mostly, not the same countries.

2. A second dataset: ND-GAIN

npm run data:vulnerability   // scripts/build-vulnerability.mjs → public/data/vulnerability.json (36 kB)
  • The ND-GAIN Country Index (Notre Dame Global Adaptation Initiative) scores each country's vulnerability: its exposure, sensitivity and capacity to adapt to the effects of climate change, across food, water, health, ecosystems, habitat and infrastructure. 0 = least vulnerable, 1 = most. 192 countries, 1995–2024.
  • It comes as a 4.9 MB zip of CSVs. The script downloads it once (pinned to the 2026 release, cached in scripts/.cache), takes one file out, keeps our countries and 3 decimals, and writes 36 kB. The page never sees the zip.
  • No zip library: a zip ends with a "central directory", one record per file with its name, size and where its data starts. Reading that and inflating the one entry with Node's own zlib (zip's deflate is raw deflate) is 30 lines.
  • Two practical surprises: the server answers 403 to clients that don't send a browser-like User-Agent, and the folder inside the zip is called resources 2.
  • It joins on ISO3 codes, like OWID (lesson 08). 48 of our 240 areas aren't scored (mostly dependencies and territories: Åland, Bermuda, the Cayman Islands…) and show as "no data", never as a colour.
  • Citation, as ND-GAIN asks (for the methodology page, lesson 19): "Notre Dame Global Adaptation Initiative Country Index (ND-GAIN). 2026."

3. Bivariate: two variables, nine colours

// charts/bivariate.ts
x = vulnerability     → 3 classes (tertiles)
y = responsibility    → 3 classes (tertiles)
colour = STEVENS_3X3[y][x]
  • Each variable is cut into thirds (tertiles): a third of the countries low, a third middle, a third high. Tertiles make two different units comparable: "high" means "in the top third", whether it's a 0–1 score or a percentage of all CO₂.
  • Responsibility is the share of all CO₂ since 1750, not this year's emissions: climate change is driven by the CO₂ accumulated in the air, so history is what counts for responsibility.
  • The palette is Joshua Stevens' standard 3 × 3: one hue per variable (teal for vulnerability, pink for responsibility) blending to dark blue-purple where both are high. Only three steps per axis: with 4 × 4 = 16 colours, nobody can read a colour back to its two classes.
  • The breaks are cut in 2024 and applied to every year, as in lesson 12, so a colour means the same thing whichever year the timeline shows. The timeline narrows to 1995–2024, the years ND-GAIN covers.
  • A country needs both values to get a colour; with one missing, it's "no data".

4. A legend for two variables

  • charts/bivariate-legend: the nine colours as a 3 × 3 grid, vulnerability rising to the right, responsibility rising upwards. A grid is the only honest key for a bivariate map: a list of nine swatches would hide which two classes each colour means.
  • Beside it, the corners that tell the story, in words: "Caused much, at little risk" (pink), "Caused little, at great risk" (teal), "Both high".
  • The metric select is now written once as an ng-template and projected into whichever legend is showing. ngProjectAs="[legend-title]" lets an ng-container fill the legends' title slot, a selector it couldn't otherwise match.
  • In the state, ?metric=divide is a view of its own, but metric() reports its responsibility side (the share since 1750). The year range, the series, the panel and the scatter's colours keep working without knowing about the divide.
