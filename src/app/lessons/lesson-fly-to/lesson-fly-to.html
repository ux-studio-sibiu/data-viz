Fly to a country

Select a country, by clicking, searching or following a link, and the globe flies there: the country ends up centred and filling about 30% of the screen. Try Japan, then Brazil, then Monaco (as close as the camera goes, so Monaco itself stays small).

Taken out of order, after lesson 07. The roadmap's "panel reveal" half of this lesson arrives with the detail panel in lesson 11.

1. Where to put the camera

globe/framing.ts answers "centred, 30% of the screen" with two numbers: a direction and a distance.

  • Centre: the country's label point from Natural Earth, which lies on its main body. A centroid of all its parts would aim at the Atlantic for France (mainland + French Guiana + Réunion).
  • Size: only the main body counts, the polygon containing the label point. Its size is an angle on the sphere, θ, the farthest outline point from the centre (d3.geoDistance).
  • Distance: from the globe's centre, a point θ away from the point under the camera appears at an angle α where tan α = sin θ / (D − cos θ). The screen's height spans 2·tan(fov/2) in the same units. Setting 2·tan α = 0.3 × 2·tan(fov/2) and solving: 
    D = cos θ + sin θ / (0.3 · tan(fov/2))
  • Clamped between 1.8 and 4. 1.8 is ZOOM.closest in globe.ts, the same limit scroll and pinch use: Europe fills the screen (Monaco would otherwise need the camera inside the Earth). 4 keeps Russia and Brazil from backing off too far. On a portrait screen the width is the tighter side, so the horizontal field of view is used.

2. How to get there: GSAP drives, Three.js draws

gsap.to(progress, { t: 1, duration: 0.7 + 0.5 · angle, ease: 'power2.inOut',
  onUpdate: () => {
    direction = slerp(from, to, t)                       // along the great circle
    distance  = lerp(D0, D1, t) + lift · sin(π · t)       // a little climb mid-way
    camera.position = direction · distance; camera.lookAt(0, 0, 0); render();
  } })
  • GSAP tweens a single number, t. Everything else is computed from it each frame. Tweening camera.position.x/y/z directly would fly in a straight line through the Earth.
  • Slerp (spherical interpolation) of a quaternion rotates the direction along the great circle from where the camera is to above the country, at an even speed.
  • The climb: lift · sin(π · t) is 0 at both ends and peaks mid-flight. Up to 0.6 radii on a trip to the far side of the globe, like a plane climbing and descending, so you see where you're going.
  • Duration from distance: 0.7 s next door, about 2.3 s to the antipode. Japan → Brazil took 2.05 s.
  • camera.lookAt(0, 0, 0) with camera.up = +Y keeps north up all the way.
  • ≈ Mapbox's flyTo, or Google Earth's "fly to". In React Three Fiber you'd do the same in useFrame, or with @react-spring/three.

3. Who owns the camera?

  • Three engines could move the camera: OrbitControls (drag), auto-rotation, and now GSAP. One at a time:
  • During a flight the render loop is paused, and GSAP's own ticker calls render(). wake() ignores pointer moves while flight is set, otherwise OrbitControls' leftover damping from an earlier drag would pull against the tween.
  • A drag interrupts: OrbitControls' start event kills the tween, and the user is in control from where the camera is.
  • A selection stops auto-rotation for good: a country you chose shouldn't rotate away.
  • Reduced motion: the camera jumps to the target, no flight.
  • No flight before the globe is shown (added after lesson 13): on load with ?country=, or coming back to the globe from the map or the scatter with a country selected, the camera starts on the country and the globe fades in already there. A flight from a view nobody saw would only be motion for its own sake. Choosing a country while the globe is on screen still flies.

4. When it flies

  • An effect on selected and features: any selection, wherever it comes from (a click, the search, the back button, a link with ?country=). Switching from the map to the globe with a country selected flies there from the start view.
  • The flight itself runs in untracked(): it reads the camera, not signals, and nothing it touches should re-run the effect.
  • Deselecting doesn't move the camera.
  • GSAP is imported only by the globe, so it ships in the globe's lazy chunk (563 → 666 kB raw, 117 → 149 kB gzipped), not with the app.

5. A testing note

  • In a hidden browser tab, requestAnimationFrame doesn't fire, and GSAP's ticker runs on it: the tween was created but stayed at frame 0. That's correct behaviour (no animating in the background), but it can look like a bug. Ticking gsap.ticker.tick() by hand showed the flight working.
