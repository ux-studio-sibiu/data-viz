The carbon divide

Who emitted the CO₂, and who pays for it

Scroll to follow 125 years of emissions, from a handful of chimneys to a warming world.

1900

A handful of chimneys

The United Kingdom burned coal for 10.2 tonnes of CO₂ per person, the United States 8.9, Germany 5.9. India: 0.04. Most of the world emitted next to nothing, or wasn't measured at all.

1950

One country, almost half

The United States alone emitted 2,541 Mt of CO₂ that year: 44% of the world's. The columns are each country's total; only the few that together emit 90% of it stand up.

2024

The centre moves east

China now emits 12,290 Mt a year, a third of the world's 37,400. The United States and India follow. Thirty-three of 212 countries emit 90% of all CO₂.

Per person

A different map

Divide by people and the order changes. An average Qatari emits 41 tonnes a year, an American 14, a Chinese 8.7, an Indian 2.2, a Nigerien 0.1: about 350 times less than a Qatari.

Wealth

Richer, higher

Plot every country by wealth and emissions and they line up: roughly, twice as rich, twice the CO₂. The exceptions show it's a choice of energy, not destiny: Switzerland and the United States are about as rich, four times apart.

The divide

Who caused it, who bears it

Since 1750 the United States has emitted 23.5% of all CO₂, China 15.4%, India 3.6%. The 42 countries that caused 80.8% of it are among the least vulnerable to climate change, in pink. The 38 most vulnerable, in teal, caused 0.09%.
